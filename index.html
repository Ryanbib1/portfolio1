Course Goals & Takeaways


1. Technology in Making

I learned that HTML isn't just for building boring corporate sites. It's a tool for creativity. I used code to make a glitch aesthetic that expressed my feelings about digital chaos.

2. Experimentation

Dr. Quigley told us to "break things," so I did. My second project was all about messing up the CSS on purpose to see what happened. It was fun to stop worrying about being perfect.

3. Workshop Environment

Sharing code with classmates helped a lot. Sometimes my code wouldn't run, and having a second pair of eyes saved me hours of frustration.

4. Practice Revision

I realized web design is never done. I constantly tweaked my World Cup site, changing colors and fixing links until the user flow felt natural.

5. Presentation

Learning to explain my code is as important as writing it. This portfolio is my way of showing not just what I built, but why I built it.

Portfolio 2: Audio & Visuals


Sound Composition

It was super fun to actually make our own sound project. I never edited audio before, so figuring out the layers and timing was a cool new challenge.

Photo Text Collage

I used pictures I took around our beautiful campus and paired them with quotes from Roland Barthes. Designing the layout was a nice break from coding.



The Podcast

This was a truly amazing project. Writing the script and recording our own voices felt totally different from writing code. It took a few takes to sound natural!

Video Photo Essay

Photo essays are my favorite. It's fun to write thoughts for the pictures and add cool transitions. Getting the timing right with the video editor was key.

Course Goals & Takeaways (Part 2)


1. Technology in Making

Using audio and video software showed me that technology isn't just about writing algorithms. It's a tool to shape how people feel when they listen to a podcast or watch a video.

2. Embrace Experimentation

I experimented a lot with sound levels and video transitions. Sometimes an edit sounded terrible, so I just had to hit undo and try something completely different until it worked.

3. Workshop Environment

Hearing my classmates' podcasts and seeing their essays gave me great ideas. We helped each other fix weird audio glitches and layout issues.

4. Practice Revision

Editing is basically debugging for media. I had to go back and trim audio clips or swap photos multiple times to make the final story flow better.

5. Present your Work

Uploading everything to SoundCloud and YouTube makes it feel real. It's cool to have a link I can actually send to my friends and family to show them what I made.
