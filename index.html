Phase 1 Course Goals


1. Foster the creative habits of mind

I've always just done assignments to get them done. But with the F1 Webtext, something just clicked. Since I actually care about F1, I started asking myself, "What would I want to see on a site like this?" It stopped feeling like a school project and more like something I was making for myself or for someone who just knows F1 and wants to know more about it.. It was the first time I felt like I was actually creating something, not just because of grades.

2. Engage with others in a workshop environment.

Showing my work to my group is kinda scary, not gonna lie Josh is surprised that I can build a website like this. When I showed off the Multi-Page Challenge, I thought my navigation was super obvious. But someone else got lost in like two seconds. My first thought was, "No, you're just using it wrong!" But then I realized I was the one who was wrong. What's obvious to me isn't obvious to everyone. It was a humbling reality check that I can't just design for myself.

3. Think deeply about craft.

My idea of craft totally changed with the Multi-Page Challenge. It’s one thing to make a single page look good, but it’s a whole other thing to make an entire site feel connected. I remember getting so frustrated because my navigation bar would be slightly different on each page, or a link would be broken. It felt messy. That’s when I realized craft isn't just about the flashy parts, it’s about consistency and making sure the whole experience feels solid for the user, not like it's about to fall apart.

4. Engage in sustained research and skills development.

I got so stuck on the Multi-Page Challenge. My links were just broken, and it was super frustrating because I had no idea why. I spent what felt like forever on Google, reading stuff that didn't make sense, until I finally found one post on web3 school that explained it. Making a website by studying on your own are actually great(using ai can also help you student if you use as a correct way)The feeling of fixing it myself was awesome. It taught me that being good at this stuff isn't about knowing all the answers, it's about being stubborn enough to find them when you're totally lost.

5. Learn to present your work

Honestly, putting this portfolio together has been its own kind of project. I thought you just put your work on a page and that was it. But when I had to actually write the descriptions for my F1 Webtext and Multi-Page Challenge, I kind of blanked. It's surprisingly hard to explain what you did and why it matters. It forced me to actually think about my process. I'm realizing that if you can't explain what you learned, maybe you haven't fully learned it yet.

Phase 2/3: Expository Documentaries and Animation Effects


Photo Essay

This was my first step into visual storytelling. I realized that arranging photos isn't just about making a slideshow; it's about creating a narrative. I had to think carefully about the order and pacing of the images to tell a story without needing a ton of words.

Video Essay

Moving from still images to full video was a huge jump. This project forced me to juggle three things at once: visuals, audio, and timing. I learned that a video essay isn't just reading a script over a video; the visuals actually have to 'speak' too.

Special Effect in Premiere Pro

I used to think special effects were only for big budget movies. In this project, I learned how to manipulate footage right on my laptop. It taught me that editing isn't just cutting clips together; it's about altering the reality of the video to make it look cooler or more professional.



Adobe After Effects

Honestly, After Effects is intimidating. It’s totally different from Premiere. This project was all about learning 'keyframes' and motion graphics. It’s tedious work—moving things frame by frame—but seeing the final animation move smoothly makes all that patience worth it.

Adobe Character Animator

This was surprisingly the most fun project. I got to bring a character to life just by using my webcam and voice. It showed me that you don't need to draw every single frame by hand to be an animator anymore. It’s all about acting the part.

Phase 2/3 Course Goals


1. Foster the creative habits of mind

I used to think creativity was just having a 'good idea.' But working with video, especially on the Video Essay, showed me that creativity is actually about problem-solving. When I didn't have the right footage, I couldn't just give up; I had to get creative with B-roll or find a different way to visualize my point. It shifted my mindset from 'I can't do this' to 'How can I work around this?'


2. Engage with others in a workshop environment

Video is weird because you watch it so many times while editing, you stop seeing the mistakes. In the workshop for my Photo Essay, I thought my pacing was perfect. But a classmate pointed out that one section was way too fast and they couldn't process the image in time. I never would have caught that on my own. It proved to me that fresh eyes are the most valuable tool in editing.


3. Think deeply about craft

My definition of 'craft' in Phase 2 is basically 'invisible editing.' When I started the Special Effects project, I wanted to make everything flashy. But I realized the best editing is the stuff you don't notice—like syncing the cut exactly to the beat of the music or fixing the audio levels so they don't jump around. Craft isn't about showing off; it's about removing the friction so the viewer just enjoys the content.


4. Engage in sustained research and skills development

Adobe After Effects is a beast. I remember staring at the screen having zero idea what to do. I couldn't just 'figure it out'—I had to research. I spent hours watching YouTube tutorials just to understand how to make a simple text animation. It was humbling, but it taught me that 'skills development' is really just the ability to not get discouraged when you have to Google something for the 50th time.


5. Learn to present your work

Presenting video is different than presenting a website. With the Character Animator project, I realized that the presentation starts before the video even plays—it's about the thumbnail and the hook. You have to convince someone to click. This portfolio has taught me that my work doesn't speak for itself; I have to frame it correctly so people understand why I made the choices I did.


Phase 4: Presentation and Interactive Installation


The Pathfinder Research Project

Pathfinderrrr! Made for college student

Phase 4 Course Goals


1. Foster the creative habits of mind

Research felt like falling down a rabbit hole. I started with one idea, but the 'pathfinder' concept made me realize I had to be flexible. Creativity here wasn't making things up; it was finding creative connections between facts that didn't seem related at first. It taught me to follow the thread wherever it leads.

2. Engage with others in a workshop environment

Presenting raw data is boring. In the workshop, I realized no one cared about my stats until I told a story with them. My classmates helped me see that I was too deep in the weeds. I had to zoom out and show why this research matters to a normal person, not just dump information on them.

3. Think deeply about craft

For an interactive installation, 'craft' is about user experience. It's not just writing a paper; it's designing a journey for the reader. If the link creates friction or the layout is ugly, people stop reading. I had to polish the digital experience just as much as the writing to make sure the research was actually accessible.

4. Engage in sustained research and skills development

This was the deep dive. I learned that a quick Google search isn't enough. I had to cross-reference, verify, and actually synthesize information. The 'Pathfinder' wasn't just a name; it was literally me trying to find a path through a mess of information to find the truth. It required a level of patience I didn't know I had.

5. Learn to present your work

This is the grand finale. Presenting this felt less like a final exam and more like a celebration of how far we've come. I honestly learned just as much watching my classmates' solutions as I did making my own. A massive thank you to Dr. Quigley and the whole class for the vibes and the feedback, I couldn't have done it without you.
